A chosen meaning

Class done.
Café next?

Side Street is a simple base for small discoveries with friends.

The primary audience hypothesis is a student moving from class to friends who wants to feel casual but put together. A secondary need checks whether the quiet base leaves room for a colorful jacket. These are design assumptions, not customer interviews.

Explorer describes the brand’s recurring character, not a personality assigned to every visitor. The name, turn cue and everyday language invite a small detour.

This is a working coaching example. The project README explains how to run this preserved example. New students should use the unfinished assignment template; SPEC.md records the decisions and actual check evidence.

Useful and truthful

Help first.
Leave the choice open.

Reciprocity

The freely available jeans-and-open-overshirt idea offers a little styling help before a visitor takes any action. It needs no signup or purchase.

Liking

Familiar class-to-café language aims to make the page feel approachable for the chosen situation. That intended connection is a design hypothesis, not social proof or a conversion claim.

Boundaries

Product and price are fictional. The working size selector and temporary demo bag do not place orders, collect payment, or persist between visits. No product-property claims or customer reviews are invented.
